# FlowForge speaker notes

## Slide 1
Start by saying FlowForge watches how work actually happens, then turns repetitive workflows into tested automation. Mention the four sponsor products as one loop: VideoDB observes, Kimi reasons, Bright Data researches, Daytona validates safely.

## Slide 2
Explain the problem simply: teams repeat tiny manual workflows every day, but those workflows are invisible until someone documents them. FlowForge starts from a screen recording, so the product sees the real behavior instead of waiting for a perfect prompt or process spec.

## Slide 3
Walk through the loop: record the work, diagnose what should be automated, pull live context from the web, then run a safe first draft. The key point is that FlowForge does not just suggest automation, it produces a runnable starting point.

## Slide 4
Use this slide to show sponsor fit. VideoDB captures workflow evidence, Kimi creates the automation plan, Bright Data handles live public web research, and Daytona gives a safe sandbox to test generated actions before anything touches production.

## Slide 5
This is the proof slide. Say the MVP already detects repetition, estimates complexity and risk, and shows how vendor research can become an automated run. Point out that the demo turns one observed manual research workflow into the next rows automatically.

## Slide 6
Explain the architecture in four layers: observe, reason, research, execute. This makes the product feel production-ready because each layer can improve independently, and the system can graduate from demo workflow to real team deployment.

## Slide 7
Close with the startup angle: FlowForge is not a chatbot, it is an AI operations analyst. The wedge is repetitive vendor research and spreadsheet workflows, then it expands into every observed workflow that a company wants to automate safely.
